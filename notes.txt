Needs to be mentioned in the final paper:
- I am not using a system prompt for any models, just a normal user prompt template
- Using a wider variety of Wikidata relations for improved robustness
- Subtle arithmetic internals may affect exact probability values: https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/results-of-model-generate-are-different-for-different-batch-sizes-of-the-decode-only-model/34878/6 
- Exact Python library and model used for equality testing: https://github.com/zli12321/qa_metrics
- RewardBert for equality testing has some conspicuous errors; give a few examples in the appendix
- Also disclaim possible issues with Wikidata queries, e.g. isolated examples where companies have multiple headquarters and only one of them is marked correct?
- Acknowledge that the "hard to guess" constraint may easily be violated systematically, even for some of the relations in the original paper. E.g. a manufactured product may include the name of its manufacturer, or a company may derive its name from its founder.
- Rather than "simulating the generation of a as an answer to q" to get the hidden probe inputs, we use the method from A.8.2, which the authors claim "had similar results" in early experiments.
- We use layer 32/42 for Gemma since it has a different number of layers, roughly proportional to 24/32 for the other models
